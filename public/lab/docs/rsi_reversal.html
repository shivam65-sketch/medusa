RSI reversal

Buy when the RSI climbs back out of “oversold”, sell short when it falls back from “overbought”, and take the profit when it returns to the middle. A bet that sharp moves partly undo themselves.

TypeMean reversion (counter-trend)
TradesLong and short (long only on spot)
EntryRSI(14) crosses back above 30 (long) or below 70 (short), at the next open
ExitRSI reaches 50, or the 2 × ATR stop; optional time exit and trend filter
On BTC 4h, 2019–2026386 trades (about 55 a year), 52% winners, held 10 candles (under 2 days) on average

The idea

After a burst of selling, prices often bounce: sellers run out, short sellers take profit, buyers see a bargain. The RSI measures how one-sided recent moves were. When it is very low, the selling has been relentless; when it turns back up from there, the strategy bets the bounce has started and holds it until the RSI is back to neutral.

The danger is the opposite of the trend follower’s: in a strong trend, “oversold” can get much more oversold. Mean-reversion strategies win often, in small amounts, and occasionally take a full loss in a market that refuses to turn.

The ingredient: RSI

The relative strength index compares the average size of up-closes with the average size of down-closes over the last 14 candles (Wilder’s smoothing, as on TradingView):

RS  = average gain ÷ average loss            (over 14 candles)
RSI = 100 − 100 ÷ (1 + RS)                   always between 0 and 100

Example: if the last 14 candles gained $300 on average on up-candles and lost $700 on average on down-candles, RS = 300 ÷ 700 ≈ 0.43 and RSI = 100 − 100 ÷ 1.43 ≈ 30. Losses have been more than twice the gains: “oversold”. Above 70 is the mirror image, “overbought”; 50 means gains and losses balance.

The rules, exactly

  1. At each close, compute RSI(14) and ATR(14).
  2. Long: RSI was at or below 30 on the previous candle and is above 30 now → buy at the next open.
  3. Short: RSI was at or above 70 and is below 70 now → sell short at the next open.
  4. Stop: 2 × ATR from the entry.
  5. Exit: a long closes when RSI reaches 50 or more (a short at 50 or less), at the next open. With “Exit when RSI reaches: the opposite level”, longs wait for 70 and shorts for 30.
  6. Optional: time exit after n candles; trend filter: longs only when the close is above an EMA of the chosen length, shorts only below it.

A winning trade, step by step

A sharp sell-off pushed RSI below 30; one candle later it was back above 30 and the strategy bought. The bounce carried RSI past 50 within a day, and the trade closed for +1.54R.

A losing trade

Same signal, different outcome: the bounce was brief, and the price kept falling into the stop. This is how mean reversion loses: the market was not “oversold”, it was in a down-trend.

RSI length14Shorter: RSI swings more, more signals. Longer: smoother, rarer extremes.

Exit when RSI reaches50“The opposite level” holds for a full swing: bigger wins, more give-back.
ATR length, stop (× ATR)14, 2.0The safety stop. Mean-reversion trades need room to breathe; too tight a stop cuts many trades that would have bounced.
Time exit after candles0 (off)Closes bounces that never come.
Trend filter EMA0 (off)For example 200: only buy dips in an up-trend and short rallies in a down-trend. Usually the most useful change.

When it works, and when it doesn’t

  • Works: sideways markets, and dips within a healthy up-trend (with the trend filter on).
  • Fails: strong trends against the trade. Crypto trends hard, especially downwards in crashes and upwards in bull runs, which hurts both sides.

The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

Earlier tests

BTC 4h, Sep 2019 – Oct 2026 (trading starts after the warm-up), default settings, with fees, slippage and real funding: 386 trades, 52% winners, −27.8%, worst drawdown 31.8%. Even before costs it lost (−9.8%). Average trade −0.08R (t = −1.61).

A win rate above 50% and still losing money is the classic mean-reversion trap: the wins were smaller than the losses. On BTC, betting against moves has not paid; the trend filter and long-only are the first things to test, with a held-back date.

Ideas to explore

  • Trend filter 200, long only: buy dips in up-trends only.
  • Daily candles, where oversold readings are rarer and bounces bigger.
  • A time exit (for example 12 candles) so failed bounces don’t drift into the stop.

Common mistakes

  • Judging it by the win rate. Look at the average trade in R.
  • Removing the stop because “it always comes back”. Sometimes it doesn’t, and one such trade can erase months.
