Glossary

The words used in the Lab, in plain language.

TermMeaning
ATR (average true range)The coin’s typical candle size: the average of each candle’s full range (including any gap from the previous close) over the last n candles. Stops placed a multiple of ATR away adapt to how wild the market currently is.
BacktestReplaying fixed trading rules on past prices to see how they would have done.
Bollinger bandsAn average with a band two standard deviations above and below it. About 95% of closes fall inside if prices behaved “normally”; a close outside is a stretch.
BreakoutThe price moving beyond a recent high or low, often taken as the start of a trend.
Buy and holdBuying once and never selling. The baseline every strategy should be compared with.
CAGRCompound annual growth rate: the steady yearly return that turns the starting money into the final balance.
CandleOne slice of time on a chart (1 minute, 4 hours…) with its open, high, low and close prices and volume.
CompoundingSizing each trade from the current balance, so gains (and losses) grow the next trades.
CrossoverOne line moving from below another to above it (or the reverse) between two candles.
DrawdownHow far the account is below its previous high. The worst drawdown is the deepest such fall.
EMA (exponential moving average)An average that weights recent candles more than old ones, so it reacts faster than a simple average.
EdgeA real, repeatable advantage: an average trade above zero after costs that isn’t explained by luck.
FeeWhat the exchange charges per fill, as a share of the traded value (taker: you take an existing order; maker: your order waits in the book and is usually cheaper).
FillAn order being executed, at a price.
FundingPayments between long and short holders of perpetual futures, usually every 8 hours. When most traders are long, longs pay shorts.
Futures (perpetual)Contracts that follow a coin’s price, can be shorted and traded with leverage, and never expire; funding keeps them close to the spot price.
Held-back period (out-of-sample)Data kept aside while choosing settings, then used once to see whether the result holds on candles that played no part in choosing.
LeverageA position worth more than the account. 3x means a 1% price move changes the account by 3%.
Limit orderAn order to buy at or below (or sell at or above) a set price; it fills only if the price comes to it.
LiquidationThe exchange closing a leveraged position because the account can no longer cover its losses.
Long / shortLong: profit when the price rises. Short: profit when it falls (only on futures here).
Look-ahead biasA rule accidentally using information that wasn’t available at the time, such as a future price. Makes backtests look far better than reality.
MACDThe gap between a fast and a slow EMA of the price, plus a “signal line” (an EMA of that gap). Crossings suggest momentum is turning.
Market orderAn order filled immediately at the best available price (here: the next candle’s open, plus slippage).
Mean reversionThe idea that a price stretched far from its average tends to come back towards it.
OverfittingSettings tuned so tightly to past data that they capture its noise rather than a real pattern; they fail on new data.
Profit factorMoney won on winning trades divided by money lost on losing ones. Above 1 = profitable.
R (R-multiple)A trade’s result divided by what its first stop risked. Losing exactly the planned amount is −1R; making three times it is +3R.
RSI (relative strength index)A 0–100 gauge of recent up-moves against down-moves. Below 30 is called oversold, above 70 overbought.
Sharpe ratioAverage return divided by its volatility, per year. Higher means steadier.
SlippageThe difference between the price you expected and the price you got, usually against you.
SMA (simple moving average)The plain average of the last n closes.
SpotBuying the coin itself: long only, no leverage, no funding.
Standard deviationHow spread out values are around their average. Used for Bollinger band width and for the t-statistic.
Stop (stop loss)The price at which a losing trade is closed to cap the loss.
Stop orderAn order that becomes active only when the price reaches a level; used here to enter on breakouts.
t-statisticHow many “luck-sized” steps the average trade is from zero. About 2 or more suggests the result isn’t just luck.
Target (take profit)The price at which a winning trade is closed.
TimeframeHow much time one candle covers.
Trend followingBuying what is rising and shorting what is falling, hoping the move continues. Wins rarely but big.
Warm-upThe candles loaded before a test’s first trade only so the indicators have settled: 1× a plain average’s length, 4× an EMA’s, 8× an RSI’s or ATR’s. The backtest form shows it for your settings.
Win rateThe share of trades that made money.
