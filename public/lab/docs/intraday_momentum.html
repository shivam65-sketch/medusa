First half-hour momentum

If the session’s first half-hour went up, buy for its last half-hour; if it went down, sell. A famous academic finding in stocks, also reported in Bitcoin.

TypeIntraday time-series momentum
Candles1 hour or shorter (5–30 minutes is best)
TradesOne a day, held for the last half-hour
SourcesGao, Han, Li & Zhou, “Market Intraday Momentum”, Journal of Financial Economics (2018); Shen, Urquhart & Wang, “Bitcoin Intraday Time Series Momentum”, Financial Review (2022)

The idea, and where it comes from

Gao and co-authors found that in US stock index funds the first half-hour’s return predicts the last half-hour’s: news and orders that arrive overnight are absorbed at the open, and traders who rebalance late in the day push the same way. Shen, Urquhart and Wang found that the first half-hour positively predicts the last half-hour in Bitcoin too, most strongly on high-volume, high-volatility days. They defined Bitcoin’s “trading day” using volume; this version uses a 24-hour clock session from 00:00 UTC.

The rules, exactly

  1. First window: from the session’s open to 30 minutes later (rounded up to whole candles). Its return = close at the end of the window ÷ session open − 1.
  2. At the close of the candle that ends 30 minutes before the session’s end: return > 0 → buy, < 0 → sell, at the next open.
  3. The position is closed at the session’s last close (the time exit).

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

Before costs the trades averaged almost exactly zero, so with the midnight-UTC clock there was nothing to trade. The papers’ effect depends on a real open and close; try the 13:30 UTC session or the minimum-move filter, and judge it on a held-back period.
