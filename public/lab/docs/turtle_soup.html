Turtle Soup (Raschke & Connors)

The anti-Turtle: when the price breaks to a new 20-day low and immediately comes back above the old low, the breakout failed, so buy. The name mocks the Turtle traders whose breakouts it fades.

TypeFailed-breakout reversal
CandlesDaily (as designed); also tested on 4 hours
TradesLong and short, held hours to days
SourceLinda Bradford Raschke & Laurence Connors, Street Smarts: High Probability Short-Term Trading Strategies (1995)

The idea

Breakout traders all buy the same 20-day high and sell the same 20-day low. When a new low doesn’t follow through, those who sold the breakout are trapped and have to buy back, pushing the price up. Raschke’s rules require the previous 20-day low to be at least four days old, so it is a real level, and enter with a buy stop at that old low after the new low is made.

The rules, exactly

  1. A candle’s low breaks the lowest low of the previous 20 candles, and that old low was made at least 4 candles earlier.
  2. If the candle already closed back above the old low, buy at the next open; otherwise place a buy stop at the old low for the next candle.
  3. Stop at the new low; target 2 × the risk; out after 5 candles at the latest.
  4. Shorts mirror it at 20-candle highs.

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

It lost even before costs, on 13 of the 14 coin-and-timeframe tests. In crypto, breakouts of 20-candle highs and lows tend to keep going, which is exactly why the works here. Fading them fights the strongest pattern these tests found.
