Fade extreme funding

A signal only crypto has. When perpetual-futures funding is unusually high, longs are crowded and paying shorts, so go short until the next funding. When it’s unusually low, go long.

SourceBitMEX Research, funding-rate studies (“XBTUSD Funding Mean Reversion Strategy”, “Funding Mean Reversions 2018”)

The idea, and where it comes from

Funding is what longs pay shorts (or the reverse) every few hours to keep a perpetual future near the spot price. A very high rate means traders are crowded long. BitMEX’s research found that extreme funding tends to precede a move against the crowd over the next 8 hours, with the best results between 1 and 2 standard deviations from the recent average. At the most extreme readings the trend often keeps going, so those are skipped. The contrarian side also receives the funding.

The rules, exactly

  1. Each funding payment is compared with the 90 before it (30 days of 8-hour funding): z = (rate − average) ÷ standard deviation.
  2. On the candle when a payment arrives: z between +1 and +2 → sell short; between −2 and −1 → buy. Beyond 2: no trade.
  3. Close after 8 hours.

Settings unchanged from the source. Binance USD-M futures for BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, BNB, DOGE and PEPE (5-minute tests: BTC and ETH) from 2019, or each coin’s listing, to 2 Oct 2026. Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

About zero before costs and losing after, on every coin. BitMEX itself reports that extreme funding has become about 90% rarer since 2016 as arbitrage traders flatten it; our earlier research also found funding fades flipping sign between periods.
