Fade the last candle

After a green 15-minute candle be short for the next one; after a red candle be long. A 2026 study found this tiny reversal in almost every Binance coin. This page shows why a real effect can still lose every cent.

TypeVery short-term mean reversion (scalping)
Candles1 minute to 1 hour (the study used 15 minutes)
TradesLong and short, dozens a day
SourceKitron & Wengrowicz, “Short-horizon mean reversion in cryptocurrency markets: a matched cross-market measurement” (2026)

The idea, and where it comes from

Comparing 183 Binance pairs with 187 US stocks on 15-minute candles, the authors found a significant reversal in 90% of the crypto pairs but in only 2.7% of the stocks, every year since 2021. The direction of the last candle is what matters, not its size. They also measured the catch: about 1.3 basis points (0.013%) a trade before costs, well short of the roughly 5 basis points a round trip costs even for the cheapest traders.

The rules, exactly

  1. At each close: a green candle (close above open) → be short; a red candle → be long; unchanged → be flat.
  2. While the signal repeats, keep the position; when it changes, close and reverse at the next open.

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

We confirmed the study’s effect: on 5-minute candles a tiny but extremely consistent edge before costs, and on 15-minute candles a positive one since mid-2024. But it is about 0.005% a trade against about 0.2% in costs, and the strategy trades hundreds of thousands of times. Every account was wiped out. Some anomalies are real and still untradeable.
