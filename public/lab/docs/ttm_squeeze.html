TTM Squeeze (John Carter)

When volatility contracts so far that the Bollinger Bands fit inside the Keltner Channels, the market is coiling. When they expand again, the “squeeze fires”: trade in the direction of momentum.

TypeVolatility breakout
CandlesAny; tested on 4 hours and daily
TradesLong and short, ATR stop
SourceJohn Carter, Mastering the Trade (2005); the momentum histogram as in the popular TradingView version by LazyBear

The idea

Volatility comes in waves: quiet periods are followed by big moves. Bollinger Bands (2 standard deviations) shrink faster than Keltner Channels (1.5 × the average range) when the market goes quiet. When they are inside the Keltner Channels, a big move is building up; when they come back out, it has started. The momentum histogram (a fitted line through price minus its recent midpoint) picks the direction.

The rules, exactly

  1. Squeeze on: Bollinger(20, 2) entirely inside Keltner(20, 1.5).
  2. The squeeze fires on the first candle it is off again: momentum above zero → buy; below zero → sell short.
  3. Exit after two candles in a row of fading momentum, or at the 2 × ATR(20) stop.

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

Promising but unproven. The daily version made money on 5 of 7 coins with shallow drawdowns, but from only about 500 trades, too few to be sure, and it lost since mid-2024. The 4-hour version was the reverse: weak overall, positive since mid-2024. Mixed signals like this usually mean luck. A forward test would tell.
