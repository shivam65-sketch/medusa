MACD crossover

Go long when the MACD line crosses above its signal line, go short when it crosses below, and flip from one to the other on every cross. A momentum strategy that is almost always in the market.

TypeMomentum / trend following, always in the market
TradesLong and short, reversing on each cross (long only on spot)
EntryMACD(12, 26) crosses its 9-candle signal line, at the next open
ExitThe opposite cross (which also opens the reverse trade), or a 3 × ATR safety stop
On BTC 4h, 2019–20261,166 trades (about 165 a year), 33% winners, held 14 candles (2⅓ days) on average

The idea

MACD measures momentum: whether the short-term trend is pulling away from the longer-term one, and whether that pull is getting stronger or weaker. When the MACD line crosses above its own average (the signal line), momentum has turned up; the strategy goes long and stays long until momentum turns down, then flips short.

Because it flips on every cross, it is in a trade most of the time and trades often. That makes it responsive, and expensive: about 165 round trips a year on 4-hour candles.

The ingredient: MACD

MACD line   = EMA(12) of the close − EMA(26) of the close
signal line = EMA(9) of the MACD line
histogram   = MACD line − signal line        (the bars)

When the 12-EMA is above the 26-EMA, the MACD line is positive: prices have been rising faster recently. Example: EMA(12) = 64,300 and EMA(26) = 64,000 give a MACD of +300. If the signal line is at +250, the histogram is +50 and MACD is above its signal: momentum is up. The cross happens when the histogram changes sign.

The rules, exactly

  1. At each close, compute the MACD line, its signal line and ATR(14).
  2. Long: the MACD line crosses above the signal line → buy at the next open.
  3. Short: the MACD line crosses below the signal line → sell short at the next open.
  4. If a trade in the other direction is open, it is closed at that same open (the position flips).
  5. Safety stop: 3 × ATR from the entry (0 = no stop).
  6. Zero-line filter (optional): only take longs while MACD is below zero (buying a dip in momentum) and shorts while it is above zero.

A winning trade, step by step

Momentum turned up at the bottom of a dip; the strategy bought and held for 21 candles until MACD crossed back down, closing near the top of the move for +1.60R and flipping short at the same moment.

A losing trade

A cross that didn’t last: momentum ticked up, faded within six candles, and the opposite cross closed the trade for −0.45R. Many MACD losses are small like this, because the next cross usually comes before the wide stop; but there are a great many of them.

Settings

SettingDefaultWhat changing it does
Fast / slow / signal EMA12 / 26 / 9The standard values since the 1970s. Doubling all three (24/52/18) gives about half the trades and follows bigger swings.
Zero-line filteroffFewer trades, only “dip” entries. Often better after costs.
ATR length, stop (× ATR)14, 3.0The safety stop for crosses that come too late. 0 removes it (then the report can’t measure trades in R).
Trade directionbothLong only: shorts are skipped and the long simply closes on a down cross.

When it works, and when it doesn’t

  • Works: markets with smooth swings that last several times longer than the averages.
  • Fails: choppy markets (it flips back and forth, paying costs each time) and very fast moves (the cross comes late).

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

Earlier tests

BTC 4h, Sep 2019 – Oct 2026 (trading starts after the warm-up), default settings, with fees, slippage and real funding: 1,166 trades, 33% winners, −4.9% over seven years, worst drawdown 23.1%. Before costs the same trades made +64.6%.

That gap is the whole story: the momentum signal has some value, but at 165 round trips a year, each paying about 0.2% in fees and slippage, the costs ate all of it. Fewer, better trades (zero-line filter, bigger timeframe, long only) are the obvious things to test, with a held-back date.

Ideas to explore

  • Zero-line filter on, on 4h and on 1d.
  • Long only on 1d: a slow momentum filter for holding BTC.
  • In your own copy: only trade crosses in the direction of a 200-EMA trend.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the costs of an always-in-the-market strategy; compare “before costs” and “after costs” in the report.
  • Reading the histogram’s size as a forecast: it describes the recent past only.
