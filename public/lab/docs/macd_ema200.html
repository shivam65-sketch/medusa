MACD + 200 EMA

The strategy from one of YouTube’s most watched trading videos: trade MACD crosses only in the direction of the 200 EMA, and only on the “pullback” side of zero, with a target 1.5 × the risk.

TypeTrend-filtered momentum (pullback entries)
Candles15 and 30 minutes in the video; any
TradesLong and short, stop and target
SourceTrading Rush, “MACD Trading Strategy tested 100 times” (YouTube): on 15-minute EURUSD charts, a 1.5 reward-to-risk and a 61% win rate over 100 trades, a claim from one manual test

The idea

The 200 EMA defines the trend. Within an uptrend, a MACD cross upward below the zero line marks the end of a pullback: momentum turning back up while price is still cheap relative to its recent average. The video takes the trade with a stop below the 200 EMA (or the recent swing) and a 1.5R target. The author notes that not every valid signal should be taken, which is discretion a mechanical rule can’t copy.

The rules, exactly

  1. Long: close above the 200 EMA and the MACD line (12/26) crosses above its signal line (9) while both are below zero.
  2. Short: close below the 200 EMA and the MACD line crosses below its signal line while both are above zero.
  3. Stop at the 200 EMA (or beyond the 10-candle swing); target 1.5 × the risk. One position at a time.

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

About zero before costs on both timeframes, losing after them, on every coin. A 61% win rate over 100 hand-picked trades is far too small a sample to tell skill from luck; over thousands of mechanical trades the rule had no edge in crypto.
