Bollinger band reversion

When a candle closes back inside a Bollinger band after closing outside it, bet that the price returns to its average. A mean-reversion strategy built on the idea that unusual stretches don’t last.

TypeMean reversion (counter-trend)
TradesLong and short (long only on spot)
EntryA close back inside the 20-candle, 2-standard-deviation band after a close outside it
ExitThe close reaches the middle band (the average), or the 2 × ATR stop; optional time exit
On BTC 4h, 2019–2026814 trades (about 115 a year), 53% winners, held 8 candles (1⅓ days) on average

The idea

Bollinger bands draw a channel around the price that widens when the market is wild and narrows when it is calm. A close outside the band is a big move by recent standards. Waiting for the next close to come back inside is the trigger: the stretch has stopped growing, so the strategy bets on a return to the middle.

The catch: in a strong trend the price can “walk the band”, closing outside it again and again. A reversion strategy keeps stepping in front of that move.

The ingredient: Bollinger bands

middle band = average of the last 20 closes (SMA 20)
upper band  = middle + 2 × standard deviation of those 20 closes
lower band  = middle − 2 × standard deviation

The standard deviation measures how spread out the closes are around their average. Example: if the 20-candle average is 64,000 and the closes typically sit 500 away from it (standard deviation 500), the bands are at 63,000 and 65,000. If prices were bell-curve shaped, about 95% of closes would fall inside; crypto has fatter tails, so closes outside happen more often than that.

The rules, exactly

  1. At each close, compute the bands (20 candles, 2 standard deviations) and ATR(14).
  2. Long: the previous candle closed below the lower band and this candle closes back above it → buy at the next open.
  3. Short: the previous candle closed above the upper band and this one closes back below it → sell short at the next open.
  4. Exit: a long closes when a candle closes at or above the middle band (a short, at or below it), at the next open.
  5. Stop: 2 × ATR from the entry. Optional: time exit after n candles.

A winning trade, step by step

One close below the band, the next back inside: the strategy bought, and the price drifted back to the average within seven candles. The win was small (+0.79R) because the middle band was close; that is typical.

A losing trade

The same pattern, but the sell-off was the start of a bigger move: the price fell straight through the stop. A loss here (−1.08R) wipes out more than one typical win, which is why a win rate above 50% still lost money.

When it works, and when it doesn’t

  • Works: range-bound markets with no strong direction, and assets that genuinely mean-revert (some currency pairs, for example).
  • Fails: trends and breakouts, where closes outside the band are the start of a move rather than its end. Crypto spends a lot of time trending.

Earlier tests

BTC 4h, Sep 2019 – Oct 2026 (trading starts after the warm-up), default settings, with fees, slippage and real funding: 814 trades, 53% winners, −55.0%, worst drawdown 58.5%. Before costs: −23.2%. Average trade −0.09R with t = −3.09.

A t-statistic of −3.16 means this is not bad luck: on BTC 4-hour candles, fading band breaks reliably lost money. That is itself a finding: it hints that moves outside the bands tend to continue. The opposite rule (trade with the break) would be a new idea to test properly, with a held-back date, not a sure thing.

Ideas to explore

  • Wider bands (2.5 or 3) and long only.
  • Daily candles.
  • In your own copy: only trade when the bands are narrow (a quiet, ranging market), or when a 200-EMA says the trend is flat.

Common mistakes

  • Treating “outside the band” as “too far, must come back”. Statistically unusual is not the same as unlikely to continue.
  • Widening the stop after losses until the losses get rare and enormous.
