Opening range breakout

Let the first minutes of the session decide the day’s direction, take one trade that way, and hold it until a big target or the session’s end.

TypeIntraday momentum, one trade a session
Candles1 to 15 minutes
TradesLong or short, at most one a day
SourcesToby Crabel, Day Trading with Short Term Price Patterns and Opening Range Breakout (1990); Zarattini & Aziz, “Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?” (2023)

The idea, and where it comes from

Toby Crabel showed in 1990 that the first minutes of a session carry a lot of information about the rest of it: the earlier a breakout of the opening range happens, the more often it holds until the close. Zarattini and Aziz made it mechanical: take the direction of the first 5-minute candle, enter at the second candle’s open, stop at the other side of the first candle, target 10 × the risk or the session’s close. On QQQ they found a 24% win rate and +0.13R a trade before costs.

An independent replication on five stock indices in 2026 reproduced that +0.13R before costs, but found it was almost exactly eaten by trading costs (four of five markets negative). Crypto has no opening bell; here the session starts at 00:00 UTC unless you change it.

The rules, exactly

  1. The opening range is the high and low of the session’s first 5 minutes (at least one candle; a session missing its first candle is skipped).
  2. Direction mode (default, Zarattini & Aziz): when the range is complete, if it closed above the session’s open → buy; below → sell. Fill at the next open.
  3. Breakout mode (Crabel): wait for the price to break the range’s high (buy) or low (sell). The engine holds one waiting order, so it goes on the side the price is nearer to and is renewed every candle; a close already beyond the range enters at the next open.
  4. Stop at the other side of the range; target 10 × the risk.
  5. One trade per session, none in its last 60 minutes; anything open is closed at the session’s end.

The first candle pointed the way and the price kept going: the rare big winner the rule waits for.

A losing trade

More typical: the price reversed through the other side of the opening range for a −1R loss.

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

Neither version showed an edge on crypto, even before costs. With a midnight-UTC “open” that nobody trades around, the first minutes say little about the rest of the day. The 13:30 UTC start is the version worth testing next.
