Bitcoin night hours (21:00–23:00 UTC)

Buy every day at 21:00 UTC and sell at 23:00 UTC. Quantpedia found that a big share of Bitcoin’s long-run gain happened in those two hours, when every major stock exchange is closed.

SourcePadyšák & Vojtko (Quantpedia), “Are There Seasonal Intraday or Overnight Anomalies in Bitcoin?” (2022); paper “Seasonality, Trend-following, and Mean reversion in Bitcoin”

The idea, and where it comes from

Looking at Bitcoin’s average return for each hour of the day from 2015 to 2022 (Gemini exchange), Quantpedia found the hours ending at 22:00 and 23:00 UTC had the largest and most significant gains. They report 33% a year for this two-hour strategy, with a 20.9% annual volatility and a −22.5% worst drawdown, with less risk than holding Bitcoin. Nobody has a solid explanation; New York has just closed and Asia hasn’t opened.

The Days setting also lets you test other calendar ideas, such as the “Monday effect” reported in early Bitcoin research.

The rules, exactly

  1. At the close of the candle ending at 21:00 UTC, buy at the next open (21:00).
  2. Sell at the close of the candle ending at 23:00 UTC (the time exit).
  3. Long only, every day (or only the chosen days, by the UTC day of the buy).

Settings unchanged from the source. Binance USD-M futures for BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, BNB, DOGE and PEPE (5-minute tests: BTC and ETH) from 2019, or each coin’s listing, to 2 Oct 2026. Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

The effect is real, and it is still there: the strongest before-cost edge of every strategy tested here, on all 7 coins, and still clearly positive after mid-2024. Our own earlier research found the same drift on 15 other coins. But it averages less than 0.1% a trade, and a taker round trip costs about 0.2%, so after costs every coin lost. It could only pay with maker (limit) orders on both sides at 0.02% each and no slippage, and even then thinly. That needs a forward test with real orders, not more backtesting.
