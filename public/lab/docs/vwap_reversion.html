VWAP band reversion

The scalper’s fade: when the price stretches far from the day’s VWAP and starts to come back, trade the snap-back towards the VWAP.

TypeIntraday mean reversion (scalping)
Candles1 to 15 minutes
TradesLong and short, a few a day
SourceA staple of intraday trading courses and trading-desk practice; there is no well-known published test of it on crypto, which is why we ran one.

The idea

The VWAP is where most of the day’s volume changed hands. VWAP bands sit a number of volume-weighted standard deviations above and below it, like Bollinger Bands around a moving average. The belief behind the fade: a price two deviations away from where everyone traded is stretched, and tends to drift back. Waiting for a candle to close back inside the band is a common filter, so you don’t step in front of a move that is still accelerating.

The rules, exactly

  1. The VWAP and its spread restart at each session’s start (00:00 UTC by default). Bands: VWAP ± 2 standard deviations.
  2. No trades in the session’s first 12 candles (the bands need data) or on its last candle.
  3. Long: the previous candle closed below the lower band and this one closes back above it. Short: the mirror at the upper band. Fill at the next open.
  4. Exit: the close reaches the VWAP, or the stop (1.5 × ATR(14)) is hit, or the session ends.

A winning trade

A stretch below the band that snapped back to the VWAP.

A losing trade

The stretch kept going: on trending days the bands widen and price walks along them.

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

It loses even before costs: in crypto, a price that has run two deviations from the VWAP tends to keep going more often than it snaps back. That fits everything else in these tests, where trend rules did better than fades.
