Turtle breakout

Buy when the price closes above its highest high of the last 20 candles, sell short below the lowest low, and stay in until the price breaks back through a 10-candle channel. Lose small and often, win big and rarely: the purest form of trend following.

The idea, and where it comes from

In 1983 the commodity traders Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt argued about whether trading could be taught. Dennis recruited a group of beginners, the “Turtles”, taught them a fixed set of breakout rules in two weeks, and gave them real money. Several made fortunes over the following years. Their core rule was this one: enter when the price breaks out of its recent range, and exit when it breaks back through a shorter range.

It works when markets trend: a break to a new 20-candle high is often the start of a move that runs much further than anyone expects. Most breakouts fail and are stopped out for a small loss, but the few that turn into long trends make many times what they risked. The win rate is low (about 30%) by design.

The version here is the Turtles’ “System 1”, simplified: one position at a time, no adding to winners, and no rule to skip the next signal after a winning trade.

The ingredients

The channel: highest high and lowest low

The 20-candle channel is the highest high and the lowest low of the 20 candles before the current one. A close above the top line means the price is higher than at any point in that window. The 10-candle channel is the same over 10 candles; being shorter, it sits closer to the price and is used for the exit.

ATR for the stop

ATR(20) is the average candle range over 20 candles (Wilder’s smoothing, as TradingView uses). The stop sits 2 × ATR from the entry, the Turtles’ “2N” stop. It caps the loss when a breakout fails right away.

The rules, exactly

  1. Long: a candle closes above the highest high of the previous 20 candles → buy at the next open.
  2. Short: a candle closes below the lowest low of the previous 20 candles → sell short at the next open.
  3. Stop: 2 × ATR(20) from the entry price.
  4. Exit a long when a candle closes below the lowest low of the previous 10 candles (a short, when it closes above the highest high of the previous 10), at the next open.
  5. No target: a winning trade runs until the exit rule ends it.
  6. No new trade on the candle a trade closes; one position at a time.

A winning trade, step by step

This is what the strategy lives for: a breakout that kept going. The exit line (grey) trailed below the price as it rose, and the trade only closed when the price finally dipped below it, locking in +2.93R. A target would have cut this short.

A losing trade

A false breakout: the price closed below the 20-candle low, the short filled, and within eight candles the price was back above the stop. Most Turtle trades look like this. The strategy accepts many of these −1R losses in exchange for the occasional +5R, +10R or more.

When it works, and when it doesn’t

  • Works: big, persistent moves in either direction (2020–2021 and 2024 in crypto), and across many markets at once, because some market is usually trending.
  • Fails: long sideways ranges, where every breakout reverses (much of 2025 for many coins). Expect long flat or losing stretches; the worst drawdown is usually much longer than you’d guess.

Earlier tests

BTC 4h, Sep 2019 – Oct 2026 (trading starts after the warm-up), default settings, with fees, slippage and real funding: 400 trades, 30% winners, +110.1% (before costs +206%), worst drawdown 20.8%, average trade +0.22R (t = 1.61). Good, but on one coin it isn’t quite conclusive (t below 2). Buy and hold made more over the same candles (+943%) with a far deeper drawdown (−77%).

The test that mattered: this exact rule set (4h, 20/10, 2 ATR), decided in advance and run once on the 30 highest-volume Binance futures, made +964R over 7,912 trades after fees (t = 4.31), +819R after funding. Re-run on a fair basket chosen quarter by quarter, including coins later delisted, it still made +554R (+0.049R a trade, t = 2.40), with every year positive except 2025. It is the only rule set in this project that passed a held-back test.

The edge is real but modest and bumpy: a fair basket at 0.1% risk per trade grew about +55% over six years with a 26% worst drawdown, and needed many positions open at once. Don’t re-tune its settings on the same data; that would undo the test.

Ideas to explore

  • Test it on several coins (import ETH, SOL, BNB…) without changing the settings, and add up the results.
  • Try 55/20 (System 2) on 4h or 1d.
  • In your own copy: add the Turtles’ rule to skip a breakout if the previous breakout trade was a winner.

Common mistakes

  • Giving up during a losing streak: ten losses in a row is normal for this strategy.
  • Adding a profit target “to lock in gains”: it removes exactly the big winners the strategy depends on.
  • Using short timeframes: on 1-minute candles the same rules lost the whole account to costs.
