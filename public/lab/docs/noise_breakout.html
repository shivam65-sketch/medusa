Noise-area breakout

Work out how far the price normally wanders from the session’s open by each time of day. Inside that “noise area” do nothing; when the price breaks out of it, trade the breakout and trail the stop.

TypeIntraday momentum with a trailing stop
Candles1 to 30 minutes
TradesLong and short, checked every 30 minutes, flat at the session’s end
SourceZarattini, Aziz & Barbon, “Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY)” (2024)

The idea, and where it comes from

Most intraday price movement is noise. The paper measures it: for each minute of the day, the average absolute move from the day’s open over the last 14 days. Those averages drawn around today’s open form a widening band, the noise area. A price outside it suggests real buying or selling pressure. On SPY from 2007 to early 2024 the authors report 1,985% after costs, 19.6% a year, Sharpe 1.33 (they also size positions by volatility, which this version doesn’t; the Lab’s own sizing is used). It won 4th place in the 2025 Quantpedia Awards.

The rules, exactly

  1. For each time slot of the session: σ = the average of |close ÷ session open − 1| at that slot over the previous 14 sessions (at least 7 must exist).
  2. Upper band = max(session open, previous session’s close) × (1 + σ); lower band = min(…) × (1 − σ).
  3. Only on candles closing at a half hour (HH:00, HH:30): close above the upper band → long; below the lower band → short.
  4. Trailing stop, checked at the same times: a long exits when the close falls below the higher of the upper band and the VWAP; a short mirrors it. It can reverse at once if the close is beyond the other band.
  5. Everything is closed at the session’s end.

Settings unchanged from the source. Binance USD-M futures for BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, BNB, DOGE and PEPE (5-minute tests: BTC and ETH) from 2019, or each coin’s listing, to 2 Oct 2026. Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

On crypto the breakouts showed no follow-through even before costs: about zero a trade. SPY has a real open and close and dealer hedging flows that crypto lacks; the midnight-UTC session here is an arbitrary line.
