RSI(2) pullback (Larry Connors)

In an uptrend, buy when a 2-day RSI says the market is deeply oversold, and sell as soon as it bounces back above its 5-day average. One of the best-known short-term rules for stocks.

TypeShort-term mean reversion within a trend
CandlesDaily (as designed); also tested on 4 hours
TradesLong and short, held a few candles, no stop
SourceLarry Connors & Cesar Alvarez, Short Term Trading Strategies That Work (2008)

The idea

Connors found that in US stocks a very short RSI (2 days) below 10 inside a long-term uptrend (above the 200-day average) marks a pullback that usually bounces within days. The quick exit, a close back above the 5-day average, takes the bounce and leaves. He argued that stops hurt this kind of strategy, so the original has none.

The rules, exactly

  1. Long: close above the 200-candle simple average and RSI(2) below 10. Short: close below the 200 average and RSI(2) above 90.
  2. Exit a long when the close is back above the 5-candle average (a short, below it), at the next open.
  3. No stop, no time limit (both can be added in the settings).

Lab default costs: 0.05% fee and 0.05% slippage on every fill, about 0.2% a round trip, plus real funding. The average trade is in % of the position; t above 2 means it is unlikely to be luck. “Profitable after costs” needs a positive average with t ≥ 2 and a profit on most coins; “Edge before costs only” means the rule predicts something, but less than the trading costs.

A small edge before costs on 4-hour candles, nothing convincing after them, and negative since mid-2024. On daily candles the average trade looks positive but rests on too few trades to mean anything. Short-term pullbacks in stocks bounce; in crypto they keep falling too often.
